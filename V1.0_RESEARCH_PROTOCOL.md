# Ω-Math v1.0 — Research Protocol

## Purpose

Ω-Math v1.0 turns the closed v0.9 typed relational language into a reproducible research interface.

The language remains intentionally small. Research machinery is layered around it rather than silently promoted into primitive mathematics.

## Research object

Every executable study is represented as:

`Study = (Hypothesis, Null, Model, Intervention, Observation, Metric, Criterion, Record)`

where each component is explicitly declared.

## Mandatory declaration

A study MUST state:

1. hypothesis identifier and version;
2. exact model/configuration;
3. admissible transformations or dynamics;
4. intervention set, if applicable;
5. observation function;
6. measured quantities and units;
7. null hypothesis;
8. success/failure criterion;
9. horizon and stopping rule;
10. random seed or deterministic generation rule;
11. software/language version;
12. expected invalid conditions;
13. provenance of generated data;
14. whether the result is Definition, Derivation, Executed, Supported, Hypothesis, Theorem, Counterexample, or Rejected.

## Separation of layers

### Layer A — Ω-Math semantics

Typed entities, relations, paths, transformations, equivalence and declared derived operators.

### Layer B — Research model

A finite or otherwise explicitly bounded model instantiated using Layer A.

### Layer C — Experiment

A deterministic or seeded procedure that executes the model and records observations.

### Layer D — Inference

Statistical or logical comparison against the preregistered criterion.

### Layer E — Interpretation

Scientific interpretation is external to the language and MUST NOT be silently inserted into Layer A.

## Null-first rule

A result is not evidence for a hypothesis merely because the hypothesis model produces a visible pattern.

The null model and comparison criterion must be declared before execution whenever the study claims empirical support.

## Counterexample rule

For universal mathematical claims, a single valid counterexample within the declared domain is sufficient to reject the claim as stated.

For empirical claims, failure in one finite model does not establish universal impossibility; it records a counterexample to the stronger claim.

## Reproducibility

Every executed study MUST permit reconstruction from its record, source version, parameters and seed/generation rule.

## Forbidden upgrades

The research protocol MUST NOT silently convert:

- relation sign into physical force;
- transformation cost into physical energy;
- transition count into physical time;
- connectivity into physical space;
- correlation into causality;
- behavioral stability into emergence.

Such bridges require independent definitions and validation.

## Research pipeline

`declare → formalize → preregister → generate → execute → observe → compare → falsify/support → record`

## v1.0 design target

The goal is not universal mathematical completeness. The goal is a small language with a strict, machine-readable boundary that can serve as the common formal substrate for Ω research, LAB experiments, Graph models and later SPACE/AGI interfaces.
