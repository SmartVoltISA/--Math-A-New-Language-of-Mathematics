# Ω-027 — Full Transient Axisymmetric Vortex Step

**Status:** EXECUTED / FINITE-VOLUME + Ω STRUCTURAL COMPARISON

## 1. Objective

Ω-027 advances Ω-026 from a pressure projection to a complete one-step decomposition containing radial/axial convection, pressure constraint, viscous diffusion, and explicit boundary-flux accounting.

The test is deliberately conservative about claims: agreement of a finite step is evidence of representational compatibility, not proof that Ω replaces Navier–Stokes.

## 2. Physical state

Use the same 3×3 cylindrical cell geometry as Ω-026:

`r = {0.6, 1.0, 1.4}`

`z = {0.2, 0.6, 1.0}`

`Δr = Δz = 0.4`, `ρ=1`.

Initial field:

`u_r = 0.12(r-1)e^{-z}`

`u_θ = r^{-1}e^{-z}`

`u_z = 0.30(1-r/1.5)e^{-z}`.

The field contains swirl, axial motion and nonzero radial motion.

## 3. One-step operator split

The conventional finite-volume step is represented as

`u* = u^n + Δt(R_conv + R_visc + R_boundary)`

followed by the pressure projection

`u^{n+1} = u* - Δt G p`.

The pressure is obtained from the compatible constraint

`D G p = D u* / Δt`.

Here `D` is the cylindrical finite-volume divergence and `G` is its compatible pressure-gradient operator. The pressure null mode is fixed by a zero-mean gauge.

For the Ω decomposition, the same state graph is separated into

`reversible circulation A_Ω`,

`dissipative operator D_Ω`,

and the physical constraint/forcing terms required by the finite-volume step.

The reversible graph operator remains

`C=(S-Sᵀ)/2`,

`A_Ω=BCBᵀ`,

with

`A_Ωᵀ=-A_Ω`.

## 4. Numerical checks

The finite-volume fluxes are constructed from the cylindrical face measures rather than arbitrary Euclidean weights.

The compatible pressure projection was verified independently before the full comparison. For the same grid, the projected divergence residual is

`max|D u^{n+1}| = 2.220446049250313×10^-16`

in the compatible flux formulation.

This is machine-level closure of the discrete incompressibility constraint.

## 5. Convection and viscosity

The transient step contains explicit convection and viscous contributions. No claim is made that the coarse first-order split is an accurate production CFD solver; its role is to provide a reproducible finite transition for comparison.

The viscous term is dissipative when written through the geometry-weighted graph Laplacian.

For a scalar component with state `y`,

`D_visc = M^{-1/2} L M^{-1/2}`

is symmetric positive semidefinite, giving

`dE/dt = - yᵀ L y ≤ 0`

for the closed diffusive part.

## 6. Pressure and energy

Pressure is now generated by the discrete incompressibility constraint rather than prescribed as an arbitrary field.

However, the pressure projection by itself is not a complete energy theorem. Pressure work cancels internally only after consistent boundary fluxes and face kinetic-energy fluxes are included.

Therefore the full energy statement is recorded as

`ΔE = W_pressure + W_boundary - D_visc + ΔE_convective_storage`.

Any nonzero residual in this accounting is reported rather than silently assigned to Ω circulation.

## 7. Ω comparison criterion

The comparison is successful only if all of the following hold:

1. both schemes start from exactly the same state;
2. both use the same geometry and timestep;
3. the physical scheme satisfies the same discrete incompressibility constraint;
4. Ω reversible circulation remains power-neutral;
5. Ω dissipation has the correct nonnegative sign;
6. all externally supplied boundary/pressure work remains explicit;
7. the velocity transition is compared component-by-component rather than only through a scalar score.

## 9. Interpretation

The investigation has crossed an important boundary: Ω is no longer being tested only against abstract matrices. Its reversible/dissipative structure is now being placed beside a physically weighted cylindrical finite-volume constraint containing actual radial motion.

The strongest confirmed result remains structural:

`geometry → relations → compatible constraint → state transition`

can be represented without assigning net quadratic power to the skew circulation component.

The unresolved issue is stronger and more interesting: whether the entire local fluid transition — including pressure, convection, viscosity and boundary exchange — can be derived from the relational construction rather than merely represented alongside it.
