# RELATION — Non-Reciprocal Geometry Falsification Protocol v1.0

**Status:** PROTOCOL / NOT YET EXECUTED

## Question

Can directed relations induce a geometry that is genuinely non-symmetric, without assuming reciprocal distance as a primitive?

## Hypothesis

A directed response relation can induce a directional path cost `D(i -> j)` such that, for at least some admissible systems,

`D(i -> j) != D(j -> i)`

while the representation remains invariant under a valid relabelling/coordinate transformation.

## Null hypothesis

Any apparent asymmetry is an artifact of measurement, decomposition, or coordinate representation; after valid transformation and controls, the inferred geometry is symmetric.

## Required controls

1. Reciprocal control with the same estimator and sampling procedure.
2. Direction-reversal control.
3. Coordinate/relabel transformation control.
4. Noise control.
5. Explicit test for whether reverse edges are actually present rather than inferred.

## Ω-Math boundary

The Ω-Math layer may represent directed entities, relations, paths, transformations and declared observations. It must not silently introduce an inverse edge, physical distance, physical time, physical space, or causal interpretation.

## Pass criteria

The result can support the hypothesis only if:

- directionality survives the declared controls;
- `D(i -> j)` and `D(j -> i)` are operationally distinct observations;
- no reverse relation is invented by the formal layer;
- the representation survives the declared coordinate/relabel transformation;
- the reciprocal control does not produce the same asymmetry under identical analysis.

## Falsification

The hypothesis is falsified for the tested model if the observed asymmetry disappears under the controls or can be generated equally by the reciprocal null.

## Important limitation

A positive result would establish only a non-reciprocal geometric structure for the tested model. It would not establish physical spacetime, universal emergence, or a universal metric.
