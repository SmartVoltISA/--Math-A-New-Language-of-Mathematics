# Ω-Math — EXPERIMENT_BOUNDARY_005

## Question

Can a boundary arise from relational dynamics when a system increasingly closes its relations inward?

## Principle

Do not define the boundary in advance as a geometric surface.

Start with entities, relations and transitions only. Search for a persistent subset separating two relational regimes.

## Minimal construction

Use a finite directed system:

`Ω_t=(E_t,R_t,D_R,t)`

At each step, relations may be rewired according to a closure rule. The rule must be explicit and must not use geometric distance.

Define a candidate closure pressure from the current relational organization. The simplest experimental control is to increase the probability that a relation remains inside an already strongly connected local structure while preserving total relation count.

## Measurements

For a candidate partition `I|B|X`, measure:

`C_in = R_II / R_total`

`C_ext = (R_IB + R_BX + R_IX) / R_total`

and persistence of the partition under perturbation.

A candidate emergent boundary requires all of:

1. increasing internal closure;
2. decreasing direct interior-exterior coupling;
3. non-zero boundary-exterior coupling;
4. persistence under perturbation;
5. behavioral change when the candidate boundary is removed or rewired.

## Important distinction

The experiment must not equate:

`e_i != e_j`

with structural importance.

Identity differences may be pure labels. A distinction is structurally relevant only if it changes relations, states, history or future behavior under the admissible tests.

## Two independent controls

### Control A — label control

Randomly permute entity labels while preserving relational structure.

Expected result: measured boundary behavior should remain invariant.

If it changes, the proposed measurement is contaminated by representation.

### Control B — topology control

Randomly rewire relations while preserving total edge count and, where possible, degree sequence.

Expected result: if boundary formation depends on organization rather than simple relation count, matched null systems should not reproduce the same persistence profile at the same rate.

## Energy channel

Energy is not yet a primitive of Ω-Math. Therefore this experiment does not claim to demonstrate energy escape.

If a future independently defined flow quantity exists, it can be tested for non-zero transfer through the candidate boundary while internal closure remains high.

## Information channel

Likewise, information must not be inserted as a physical primitive here.

The first test is purely relational:

- count internal distinguishable states;
- count externally distinguishable states under a specified observation map;
- determine whether the candidate boundary acts as a stable quotient interface.

## Falsification conditions

The boundary hypothesis fails if:

- no persistent separation appears;
- the separation is entirely an artifact of labels;
- comparable separation appears under null rewiring;
- high internal closure does not systematically reduce external coupling;
- removing the proposed boundary does not alter behavior.

## Status

`PROTOCOL`

No result is claimed until the simulation is executed and recorded.
